## Ownership and update order

Runtime owns SecondarySystem. Each selected character has SecondaryVisual and FacialPerformance. SecondaryVisual owns two dynamic BufferGeometries and two materials. SecondaryMaterials owns one shared 64×256 RGBA hair texture; cloth borrows HUMAN's woven maps. Characters retain their original rig, skinned geometry and materials. Secondary visuals do not allocate Rapier bodies or change the existing body baseline.

Before HUMAN pose generation, restore the previous facial bone offsets. Run the original animation/IK, then EUPHORIA mapping/partial sole correction. FacialPerformance applies fresh bounded head/eye/jaw and morph output; head offsets are suppressed during a full physical fall and active hand interaction. SecondaryVisual reads those final bone matrices and advances world-space particles before rendering. This ordering prevents previous additive rotations from accumulating and ensures hair follows the final head, including falls.

The solver caps an update to 0.1 seconds and divides it into steps no larger than 1/120 second, with seven constraint sweeps. Root transforms interpolate through those substeps. A root translation over 1.5 metres or an update gap over 0.3 seconds resets particles to transformed rest state to avoid teleport explosions. This bounded policy does not make low-FPS time loss disappear; it follows the engine's simulated time.

## Model and mathematics

The distance solve follows the compliant XPBD multiplier update: `alpha = compliance / h²`, `deltaLambda = (-C - alpha * lambda) / (wa + wb + alpha)`, with accumulated lambda inside each timestep and mass-weighted position corrections. Structural/shear links and longer second-neighbour links use separate compliance. Second-neighbour distances approximate bending; they are not the general elastic shell formulation. See [Macklin, Müller and Chentanez, XPBD](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/XPBD.pdf).

External wind is an acceleration approximation. Wetness raises effective particle mass/damping, reduces wind influence and strip width, and changes hair/cloth roughness. Exponential rest-shape tethers are explicit artistic stabilizers, especially for fitted clothing and short hair. Parameters are DESIGN CHOICES, not fabric laboratory measurements. The solver reports finite state, actual constraint count, step/reset/collision counts and relative distance error.

Collision volumes follow torso, head, upper arms and thighs. Free particles project outside capsules and above a sampled floor. Pinned vertices stay attached; roots are not solved against collisions. Broad-phase capsule bounds reject distant particles before the distance calculation. Capsules are sampled at rendered-pose cadence, not swept through every intervening body transform. Fast rotation may still cause intersections. Contact projection does not feed forces back into Rapier.

## Rendering and graphics increment 06

A shared original alpha fibre texture modulates ribbons, with alpha testing, double-sided shadow casting and anisotropic specular material. The old rigid hair volume is reduced. Dynamic panel normals recompute after deformation; border vertex colours and shared normal/roughness maps give the drapes visible edge and weave detail. Raincoat panels add a clearcoat response. Cast members exercise different panel and hair presets simultaneously.

Five added relative morph targets have nonzero displacements in their intended regions. Brow, cheek and mouth geometry is still procedural and stylized. Front/back/face inspection views permit checking actual shapes and motion. The character inspection spawn moves off a decorative non-colliding curb that previously hid the shoes; this does not add physical curbs throughout the city.

The current scene remains far below GTA VI/RDR2-level visual fidelity. No screenshots or feature counts establish the final quality target. See GRAPHICS_PROGRESS and executable evidence for measured increments and defects.

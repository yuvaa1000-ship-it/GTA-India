# PHOTON technique inventory

This document describes the implementation present in the PHOTON source. It is not a performance report or proof that every optional path works on every browser. Runtime verification, measured costs, and the final milestone classification belong in [SYSTEM_STATUS.md](SYSTEM_STATUS.md), [RENDERING_SPEC.md](RENDERING_SPEC.md), and the browser evidence recorded for this operation. No browser verification numbers are asserted here.

**CONFIRMED FACT** below means directly inspectable behavior in the installed Three.js implementation or this repository. **DESIGN CHOICE** identifies the project's selected technique or budget. **EXPERIMENTAL IMPLEMENTATION** identifies a prototype approximation with unresolved quality limits. No competitor engine implementation is inferred or claimed.

## Executable pipeline and feature truth

The executable backend remains Three.js `WebGLRenderer` and WebGL2. `capabilities.js` checks for the float color-buffer extension and an actual complete half-float framebuffer. Its separate WebGPU inspection requests an adapter and records the returned information; it does not instantiate a WebGPU renderer, request a device, execute WGSL, or prove compute support.

The `Photon` service coordinates these stages:

1. Update authored lighting/weather parameters, the local material lab, cloud modulation, and the limited facade LOD policy.
2. Refresh the local cubemap/PMREM when marked dirty and streaming is ready. Render a view-dependent planar reflection on its configured schedule when the lab is near and its bound intersects the camera frustum.
3. Render the scene into a color target and readable depth texture.
4. Periodically sample scene luminance into a small meter target, read it back, and adapt exposure.
5. Apply a combined image-space pass for edge softening, optional AO, height fog, optional bloom, ACES filmic tone mapping, and display color conversion.

## Reflection hierarchy

**DESIGN CHOICE:** use one filtered environment capture for broad material response, then spend a second scene render only on the small horizontal puddle region. The implementation does not implement SSR or trace reflection rays against scene geometry.

The local capture is a cube camera at the lab's `probePosition`, approximately `(0, 2.4, -6)`. The capture clears existing environment assignments while rendering so a weather change does not recursively bake the preceding condition's IBL into the new probe. The planar surface is hidden during the capture. Successful filtering becomes `scene.environment`, and reflective lab materials receive the same filtered texture explicitly. Failure restores the previous environment and material assignments. PMREM targets are reused when possible.

The shader correction intersects a specular ray with the authored box from `(-12, 0, -18)` to `(12, 16, 8)`, then looks up the cubemap in the direction from the probe to that intersection. Three's roughness-dependent PMREM lookup still filters the result. Diffuse IBL is not box corrected. The world-position hook is currently intended for the lab's merged geometry; it omits per-instance transforms and must be extended before applying it to world `InstancedMesh` batches.

The probe is a snapshot, refreshed for condition/quality changes after the streaming readiness check. The first cube face refreshes the current sun shadow map once; subsequent faces reuse it. Moving pedestrians, vehicles, player geometry, and evolving cloud attenuation can therefore become stale in its reflections. The scene-wide environment assignment also means distant world materials use a plaza capture. Box correction is restricted to selected lab materials and its bound; this is not a correct local-lighting solution for every streamed cell. A probe grid, spatial blending, capture scheduling by importance, dynamic-object exclusion, and separate sky/global fallback are future work.

The puddles share one horizontal reflected-camera target at approximately `y=0.09`. The shader masks two irregular elliptical patches within that plane. It perturbs projected lookup coordinates with animated sine ripples, combines the center and four neighboring samples, and increases reflection toward grazing angles using a Schlick-shaped response with a water-like normal-incidence coefficient. Its additional minimum reflection weight and alpha blend are artistic choices, not a complete water BRDF or energy-conserving layered-water solution. The shader's fixed roughness parameter controls its small blur radius; rain primarily controls ripple amplitude. This is not convolution by the exact microfacet lobe.

When planar reflections are enabled, the lab's separate rectangular physical-water coupons are hidden. Disabling planar reflections reveals those physical-material coupons so the probe-only material path can be inspected. Their rectangular outlines are intentional fallback test surfaces, not procedural shorelines. There is no refraction of geometry underneath the planar puddle or simulation of water depth.

The planar schedule depends on quality, distance, lab visibility, and a coarse frustum bound. It can display a previously captured image between updates. Rapid camera motion may reveal lag, projection stretching, or edge artifacts; the visibility bound can also retain work when only its outer region intersects the view. A planar result cannot reflect curved surfaces or objects hidden from its reflected camera. Probe and planar captures can multiply scene work even when their target resolution is small.

## Material differences and the validation scene

All display forms and microtextures are original procedural assets. The stationary car and three-wheel auto-rickshaw are material test props; they do not implement driving, tire contacts, suspension, or physical three-wheel dynamics. The abstract bust tests surface response; it is not a finished character, animation system, or evidence of gameplay. The shallow room behind the glass is a render test distinct from GENESIS's interactive depot.

The procedural library generates four small repeating data textures for grain normal, grain roughness, flake normal, and weave normal. Normal maps are constructed from finite differences of deterministic height samples. They perturb shading, not mesh silhouettes or physical contact. Data remains in linear/no-color space, with mipmaps, trilinear minification, and anisotropic filtering capped by the renderer capability. There are no scanned maps, generated-image assets, texture arrays, KTX2/Basis streams, displacement maps, or parallax/height effects in this library.

Most static display geometry is transformed once and merged by material. This reduces draw submissions at the cost of coarser culling and shared material behavior. Reusable meshes and textures have explicit lifetime owners; there is no automatic simplification of the lab's curved forms.

## Exposure, atmosphere, shadows, and postprocessing limits

**CONFIRMED FACT:** the installed Three renderer bypasses tone mapping for ordinary offscreen render targets. The final postprocess material performs tone mapping and display conversion once. Reflective targets therefore carry linear scene color rather than an already display-tonemapped image when HDR is available.

The meter samples scene color into a `16 × 16` byte target at an interval, encodes log luminance over a bounded range, and averages the decoded values on the CPU. The exposure target is middle-gray inspired and clamped; adaptation is faster when reducing exposure than increasing it. This is a low-resolution sample estimate, not an exact image-wide luminance reduction or a camera exposure standard. Meter readback uses one WebGL2 pixel-pack buffer and a nonblocking GPU fence, polled by later frames. Pending buffers/sync objects are released on completion, timeout, failure, and disposal; no synchronous pixel read is used for exposure. GPU transfer and command submission still have a cost; bright-sign coverage, dark interiors, and quantization can influence adaptation. Metering uses scene color before the final fog and bloom pass, so it does not meter their completed appearance.

Authored directional and local light parameters use Three's physical-material lighting conventions and distance attenuation for point/spot lights. Their chosen values are artistic scene parameters, not a calibrated reconstruction of Indian sunlight, camera exposure, or measured fixture photometry. The interior point light and car headlight spots do not cast their own shadow maps. The sun map follows the player but has a fixed orthographic coverage region; shadow bias, distant coverage loss, aliasing, and movement shimmer remain relevant limits.

Height fog uses the primary depth buffer. Transparent glass and the planar water overlay do not provide physically layered depth for this pass, so foreground transparency can receive fog based on geometry behind it. Depth-derived AO has the usual screen-edge, silhouette, disocclusion, thin-geometry, and hidden-surface limitations. It darkens the combined scene color rather than performing a rigorously separated indirect-diffuse visibility calculation. There is no AO history buffer or temporal stabilization.

The always-on edge softening mixes neighboring colors according to local contrast. It can blur small text, sharp highlights, and thin details, and it cannot reconstruct coverage or stabilize subpixel motion. The main scene color target is not multisampled. Canvas antialiasing does not change that target's sampling. Bloom is intentionally restrained, but its fixed screen-space radius changes perceived size with resolution and has no energy-conserving glare model.

Rain is a compact visual test field tied to the plaza condition, not a streamed weather system. Vertex wrapping can produce visible periodicity or wrap discontinuities. Cloud modulation is similarly a test pattern: it does not derive from the procedural sky's visible cloud geometry, because no such cloud geometry exists.

## Quality controls, fallback, and ownership

These values are **configured settings**, not measured performance or quality guarantees:

| Profile     | Render scale | Sun shadow map | Planar target | Planar update interval | AO  | Bloom | Glass                 |
| ----------- | -----------: | -------------: | ------------: | ---------------------: | --- | ----- | --------------------- |
| Performance |         0.70 |           512² |          192² |      Every third frame | Off | Off   | Alpha fallback        |
| Balanced    |         0.85 |          1024² |          320² |     Every second frame | Off | On    | Alpha fallback        |
| Reference   |         1.00 |          1024² |          512² |            Every frame | On  | On    | Physical transmission |

Device pixel ratio is separately capped before applying render scale. Adaptive resolution is optional and starts disabled. Its present controller reacts to a sampled frame duration at a cooldown interval, lowering or raising the scale within bounds. Startup compilation, probe capture, and other transient stalls can influence it; it should not be described as a robust predictive scaler.

If the half-float framebuffer check fails, scene color and planar reflection use unsigned-byte targets and local PMREM capture is skipped. This avoids invoking Three's half-float PMREM targets on a device that failed the capability check. Direct lights, hemisphere fill, and the LDR planar route remain the intended fallback. LDR color clips highlights before final exposure/tone mapping, and the HDR bloom threshold cannot recover clipped brightness. A fallback branch in source is not proof that the branch has been exercised successfully on a distinct unsupported device.

| `Atmosphere`               | Owns the procedural sky mesh/material; borrows the runtime sun and hemisphere light.                                                                                               |
| `GPUProfiler`              | Owns pending timer queries and deletes them on completion/disposal. CPU submission time is recorded separately from asynchronous GPU elapsed time.                                 |
| Runtime / inherited assets | Continue to own the WebGL renderer, sun/shadows, streamed cell resources, physics, player, depot, and shared original world assets.                                                |

The transmission pass and shadow internals allocated by Three remain renderer-managed. Resource allocation counts are not total VRAM measurements. Per-pass GPU values are the latest available asynchronous samples, not synchronized averages for a single frame; exports include sample ages and hide the planar value when that pass is inactive, and must not be blindly summed with CPU duration. Draw/triangle counters include the work submitted across the rendering passes after the frame counter reset; they are not unique visible object counts.

The geometry policies in `lod.js` cover the requested protagonist, NPC, crowd, vehicle, facade, interior, vegetation, and prop categories. See [GEOMETRY_BUDGET.md](GEOMETRY_BUDGET.md) for the ceilings and error assumptions. Only the facade window-batch visibility integration currently consumes those policies during rendering. The repository does not contain the corresponding final hero assets, generated simplification chains, or an HLOD/impostor system.

Next rendering research should be driven by captured artifacts and pass timings: validate probe locality and capture scheduling; compare the optional AO and transmission against their cost; improve geometric AA/temporal stability; and test an SSR fallback only after the current reflection hierarchy is stable. Authored character/vehicle production belongs to the later operations; PHOTON's stationary coupons are not a substitute for them.
